Foundations · no robotics background assumed

Four ideas make the system understandable.

GazePick joins four mature topics: pick-and-place manipulation, human reference, eye tracking, and vision-language-action policies. This page builds each concept separately, then shows why uncertainty and execution authority must connect them.

01 · Physical task

Pick-and-place is a chain, not one action

A successful trial requires the right object and destination, a reachable grasp, collision-free transport, stable retention, accurate placement, and release. A single “success” label hides where the system failed.

Ground

Select the intended source object and destination from the visual scene.

Pick

Approach a grasp pose, close the gripper, and verify that the intended object is retained.

Transport

Move within a constrained workspace while maintaining clearance and grasp stability.

Place

Align with the destination, release, retreat, and verify final state.

Separate intent and mechanics

Let \(o^\star\) be the operator-intended object, \(\hat o\) the system-selected object, and \(y_{\mathrm{mech}}\) the mechanical outcome. Then referent correctness and task success are distinct:

\[y_{\mathrm{ref}}=\ind{\hat o=o^\star},\qquad y_{\mathrm{task}}=y_{\mathrm{ref}}\cdot y_{\mathrm{pick}}\cdot y_{\mathrm{transport}}\cdot y_{\mathrm{place}}.\]

A robot can grasp the wrong object perfectly. GazePick treats that as an intent failure, not a successful pick.

02 · Human reference

Words describe; gaze deictically points

A referring expression lets one person identify an entity for another. Attribute-rich language works when objects differ; deictic expressions such as “this one” depend on context, gesture, gaze, or a shared display.

Do not infer more than the interaction defines: looking at an object can mean selecting, inspecting, avoiding, or simply scanning it. Gaze becomes a referent cue only inside a time-bounded task state with an utterance and explicit confirmation protocol.

03 · Eye tracking

Tobii estimates gaze from reflected light

Screen-based eye trackers illuminate the eyes, image the pupils and corneal reflections, and use a calibration model to estimate a point on the display. The output is a measurement with validity and error—not direct access to attention or intent.

Sample

A timestamped \((x,y)\) display coordinate, often with per-eye data and a validity indicator.

Fixation

A derived period of relatively stable gaze. Its definition depends on the algorithm and thresholds.

AOI

An area of interest, such as a rendered object mask. An AOI hit is geometric overlap, not proof of cognition.

Quality

Calibration error, tracking ratio, sample age, head pose, glasses, illumination, and screen geometry affect usability.

04 · Coordinate systems

The gaze point lives on the screen, not in the robot world

The operator looks at the rendered camera image. Browser scaling, letterboxing, display DPI, camera distortion, and changed layout can all move the mapping. This transform must be logged and tested rather than assumed.

Display coordinate

Tobii returns \(g_d=(x_d,y_d)\) in device- or display-normalized coordinates. Convert it to physical display pixels using the active monitor geometry.

Rendered image rectangle

At gaze time \(t\), log the exact image rectangle \(R_t=(x_0,y_0,w_r,h_r)\). Reject points outside the active robot feed.

Image coordinate

Undo scaling and letterboxing to obtain \((u,v)\) in the source camera frame of width \(W\) and height \(H\).

\[u=W\frac{x_d-x_0}{w_r},\qquad v=H\frac{y_d-y_0}{h_r}.\]

Object evidence

Integrate a gaze probability density over candidate masks in the same frame. Three-dimensional ray casting is unnecessary for a fixed screen-camera view unless the task later requires depth-specific subparts.

Common implementation bug: using CSS element dimensions while the video is rendered with object-fit: contain. The invisible letterbox must be removed before mapping gaze to image pixels.

05 · Vision-language-action

A VLA maps observations and words to robot actions

A VLA combines visual perception, language conditioning, and action prediction in one learned policy. It differs from a VLM because its output is not merely text or a visual answer; it represents robot control.

Generic gaze-conditioned policy
\[a_{t:t+H}\sim\pi_\theta\!\left(\,\cdot\mid I_t,\;s_t,\;\ell,\;\phi(G_{t-W:t})\right),\]

where \(I_t\) is the camera image, \(s_t\) is robot state, \(\ell\) is the instruction, \(G_{t-W:t}\) is a recent gaze window, \(\phi\) is a gaze representation, and \(a_{t:t+H}\) is an action or action chunk. GazePick can begin with a visual prompt \(\phi(G)\) rendered as a contour or heatmap, then add a learned adapter only if data and evaluation justify it.

Direct action policy

The model emits end-effector deltas and gripper commands. This is closest to VLA literature but demands action-space alignment, rate control, and extensive physical validation.

Skill-conditioned policy

The model selects source, destination, and a parameterized pick/place skill. This is safer and easier for a hackathon, but the team must state that low-level motion is classical.

Hybrid ladder

Use a guarded skill MVP first, frozen VLA prompting second, and robot-specific action adaptation only after synchronized demonstrations are trustworthy.

06 · Uncertainty

A probability distribution should change the decision

Showing a heatmap but always choosing the maximum candidate is not uncertainty-aware. The system must estimate quality, confidence, and ambiguity, then abstain under prespecified conditions.

Original grounding model. The top candidate is not enough; the system also checks absolute confidence, top-two separation, and gaze quality.

Candidate posterior

\[\tilde p_j=\underbrace{\int_{M_j}\mathcal N(z;\mu_g,\Sigma_g)\,dz}_{\text{gaze–mask overlap}}\;\underbrace{p(o_j\mid \ell,I)}_{\text{language/vision compatibility}},\qquad p_j=\frac{\tilde p_j}{\sum_k\tilde p_k}.\]

Accept candidate \(j^\star=\arg\max_j p_j\) only when gaze quality passes, \(p_{j^\star}\ge\tau_p\), and the top-two margin \(p_{(1)}-p_{(2)}\ge\tau_m\). Thresholds are tuned on pilot data and frozen before evaluation.

07 · Guarded autonomy

Intent confidence is not motion safety

Referent uncertainty, learned-policy uncertainty, and physical risk are different. A confident target can still have an unsafe path; a safe path can still reach the wrong target.

Intent guard

Validate gaze quality, target posterior, top-two margin, destination, and human confirmation.

Motion guard

Validate workspace, joint limits, speed, acceleration, gripper state, collision margin, fresh observations, and controller mode.

Physical protection

Use low speed, barriers or marked exclusion zone, trained supervision, dead-man behavior, and a reachable E-stop.

Research claim only: the project may demonstrate fewer wrong picks under a restricted protocol. It does not certify industrial safety, replace a workcell risk assessment, or make Tobii/VLA a protective safety component.
