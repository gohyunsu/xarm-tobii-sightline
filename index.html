Selective gaze–language grounding · physical robot evidence

Look at the part.
Say where it goes.

GazePick studies one precise decision: when several similar objects match an instruction, which object does the operator mean? Tobii gaze and language form uncertain evidence over candidates. The system accepts, clarifies, or abstains before a guarded xArm moves. VLA control is evaluated separately so its value is not confused with better target selection.

90-second visual tour

Follow one instruction from ambiguity to evidence

The project is easier to understand as a chain of six decisions. Each stage has a different input, output, and failure mode; skipping a stage makes the final result impossible to interpret.

Original learning diagram. Read left to right: scene ambiguity is resolved into a candidate probability, converted to a proposal, checked independently, executed, and scored at both reference and mechanical levels.

Before motion: resolve the referent

The system asks which visible object the operator means. Tobii and language reduce ambiguity, but uncertainty can still remain. This stage ends in an accepted referent, a clarification, or abstention.

During motion: control permission

A guarded skill or learned policy proposes how the task may proceed. Confirmation and the xArm guard independently decide whether that proposal is fresh, reachable, bounded, and permitted in the restricted experiment.

After motion: explain the result

Referent correctness, grasp mechanics, placement, time, clarification, and policy condition are stored separately. That distinction reveals whether reference, action policy, or robot mechanics caused the outcome.

Motivating problem

“Pick the part” fails when the parts look alike

In the intended manufacturing and logistics scenario, similar fasteners, connectors, cartons, or subassemblies may share one camera view. A short instruction can then leave several plausible referents, while a mechanically correct grasp of the wrong object is still an intent failure.

Ambiguity

Language under-specifies

“Pick the silver connector” can match three instances. Longer descriptions require shared spatial vocabulary and may still leave more than one candidate.

Interaction question

Clicking is the practical reference

An explicit click provides a strong target cue. The study must measure whether gaze reduces manual input or decision time enough to justify its additional uncertainty and repair steps.

Physical consequence

A wrong referent becomes a wrong pick

A policy may execute a mechanically valid grasp on the wrong object. Motion success and reference success must therefore be scored separately.

Target scene

Three visually similar parts are arranged in a fixture; a blue and an orange destination tray sit within the xArm workspace. The operator looks at the middle part and says, “Put this in the blue bin.” The system estimates a distribution over all visible candidates, highlights its proposed referent, requests confirmation when needed, then performs a constrained pick-and-place.

Application-validity gate: this is currently a motivating scenario, not evidence about regional prevalence or productivity. Partner interviews and a representative workcell observation must confirm the objects, current interaction, error consequence, and acceptable timing before industrial-benefit claims are made.

Research questions

Reference first, action second

The study becomes interpretable when target selection and motion generation are treated as different causal questions. The first is the core contribution; the second is entered only after the reference interface works.

Primary · Under controlled visual ambiguity, can calibrated gaze–language fusion reduce wrong-referent activation at a useful decision coverage compared with language only and nearest-object gaze snapping?

Secondary · With the accepted referent held fixed, do frozen or xArm-adapted VLA policies improve held-out placement enough to justify their data, compute, latency, and complexity over a guarded skill?

Population
Operators assigning short tabletop pick-and-place jobs from a robot-camera screen.
Ambiguity
Two or more plausible source objects remain after interpreting the utterance.
Core intervention
Calibrated gaze–language candidate inference with accept, clarify, and abstain outcomes.
Primary outcomes
Wrong-referent activation risk, coverage, and target-decision time.
Policy ablation
Guarded skill, frozen VLA, and adapted VLA using the same accepted referent.

System semantics

Each signal answers one question

Scientific and safety confusion begins when gaze, language, policy, and robot authority are treated as interchangeable. GazePick keeps their meanings and failure modes separate.

Original role map. Gaze is evidence about a referent, not proof of intention, consent, or safety.
Non-negotiable boundary: a fixation never authorizes motion. The interface must expose the proposed target and either receive explicit confirmation or follow a separately approved activation protocol. Workspace checks, speed limits, collision constraints, watchdogs, and the physical E-stop remain independent.

One complete episode

From looking to verified placement

The task is intentionally narrow. A credible end-to-end episode is more valuable than a broad demo with unclear ground truth.

Calibrate and validate

Record Tobii calibration error at task-relevant screen locations. Confirm that the camera image rectangle and any letterboxing are known.

Present an ambiguous scene

Randomize candidate identities, layout, and destination positions while keeping all objects inside the guarded xArm workspace.

Acquire gaze and speech

Aggregate valid gaze samples over a short, preregistered window around the referring utterance; timestamp audio and transcript.

Estimate the referent

Integrate the gaze distribution over candidate masks, combine it with language compatibility, and expose confidence plus the top-two margin.

Confirm or clarify

Highlight the proposed source and destination. If quality, confidence, or margin fails, ask the operator to look again, speak more specifically, or use the click fallback.

Plan and execute

A guarded skill or a gated learned policy produces a motion proposal. The xArm guard validates the path and executes at restricted speed.

Verify and label

Record referent correctness, grasp, transport, placement, latency, clarifications, intervention, and all quality/fault states.

Research position

Selective reference is the defensible center

Gaze2Act already demonstrates gaze-conditioned real-robot VLA manipulation, while Point-VLA and VP-VLA establish explicit visual prompting for referential precision. GazePick therefore centers measurement uncertainty, selective decision-making, and matched physical baselines.

Original evidence synthesis. The center is a hypothesis space; it becomes a contribution only after the benchmark supports it.
C1 · Core

Calibrated screen gaze

A reproducible Tobii-to-render mapping with per-session error, sample quality, and candidate-level probability.

C2 · Core

Selective fusion

A gaze–language decision that accepts, clarifies, or abstains according to validation-fit confidence and top-two ambiguity.

C3 · Evidence

Matched physical baseline

A fixed-skill comparison of language, nearest-gaze snap, selective fusion, and explicit click on the same xArm scenes.

C4 · Conditional

Policy value

A second-stage comparison asking whether frozen or adapted VLA control adds held-out value once the referent is fixed.

Claim discipline: if R2 does not beat R0/R1 at useful coverage, there is no demonstrated gaze–language contribution. If P0 matches P1/P2, the correct conclusion is that VLA was unnecessary for this task. If P2 does not beat P1 on held-out trials, do not claim a fine-tuning benefit.

Definition of success

A reliable demonstration with interpretable evidence

The hackathon output should be legible in one live episode. The research output must also separate target inference from robot mechanics, expose selective failures, and show whether any VLA is earning its complexity.

Minimum credible result

  • Physical xArm completes guarded pick-and-place from at least two ambiguous layouts.
  • Target proposal and confidence are visible before execution.
  • Invalid or ambiguous gaze triggers clarification, not motion.
  • Language-only, classical gaze, and gaze-prompt conditions share matched trials.
  • Wrong-object and end-to-end success are scored separately.
  • A synchronized episode can be replayed from raw inputs to outcome.

Strong research result

  • Held-out objects, layouts, and users are evaluated.
  • Posterior confidence is calibrated, not merely ranked.
  • LoRA/OFT adaptation is compared with frozen prompting and a narrow imitation-learning baseline.
  • Latency and clarification cost are reported alongside accuracy.
  • Failure cases are categorized and reproduced.
  • Claims are stated conditionally and tied to prespecified contrasts.
Project fit: strong for an AI × intelligent-manufacturing-robot hackathon, provided the application scenario is validated with the matched team. The interaction is immediately legible, the physical demo is bounded, and the two-stage evaluation can distinguish a useful interface from a technology bundle.
