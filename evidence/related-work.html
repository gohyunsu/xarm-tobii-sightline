The closest work changes what we may claim.

Gaze-conditioned action and visually prompted VLA grounding are already active research directions. This map treats Gaze2Act as the nearest system, then adds Point-VLA, VP-VLA, GazeVLA, gaze interfaces, calibration, selective prediction, datasets, and xArm deployment to define a narrower contribution.

Evidence verdict

Novel as a selective decision boundary, not as a combination

The topic is meaningful and hackathon-ready. Its strongest paper direction is calibrated screen-based reference with a reject option. VLA adaptation is a separate, conditional contribution rather than part of the minimum novelty claim.

Defensible candidate contribution

  1. calibrated screen-to-camera Tobii uncertainty, including render geometry and per-session error;
  2. selective gaze–language grounding that accepts, clarifies, or abstains;
  3. physical xArm interface comparison with one fixed guarded skill;
  4. held-out risk–coverage evidence under similarity, separation, layout, user, and tracking quality;
  5. a second-stage policy comparison only if frozen/adapted VLA gates pass.

Claims the project cannot make

  • first gaze-conditioned VLA;
  • first visually prompted or gaze-based robot object selection;
  • first combination of gaze, language, and manipulation;
  • general industrial safety, productivity, or regional relevance before field validation;
  • fusion benefit without beating nearest-object gaze snapping;
  • VLA benefit without beating a guarded skill on the same referents;
  • fine-tuning benefit without held-out gain over the frozen policy.
Strongest framing: “When does calibrated, screen-based gaze improve ambiguous reference at useful coverage, and—once the referent is fixed—when is a VLA worth more than a transparent guarded skill?”

Closest prior work

Gaze2Act is the required comparison

Released in May 2026, Gaze2Act uses gaze for object-, part-, compositional-, and dynamic-intent conditioning in a VLA and evaluates 16 real-robot tasks on Unitree G1. It combines cross-view grounding, perception-level prompting, and action-level conditioning.

Research families

Six lines converge on the project

Each family supplies a component or baseline. None alone supports the complete GazePick claim.

Original evidence map. The central claim depends on selective R2 comparisons; VLA claims depend separately on P0/P1/P2.

Gaze-conditioned action

Gaze2Act and gaze-regularized VLA work test where gaze enters a policy. They define the closest architectural overlap.

Gaze robot interfaces

GazeGrasp, GAMMA, and assistive interfaces show gaze-based object/skill selection without the same VLA adaptation question.

Generalist VLA

RT-series, Open X-Embodiment, Octo, OpenVLA, and OFT establish large-scale pretraining and robot adaptation.

Imitation learning

Diffusion Policy is a necessary narrow-task baseline because a large VLA does not automatically win on precise single-domain skills.

Visual grounding

OWL-ViT, Grounding DINO, and SAM provide candidate detection/segmentation but do not resolve human intent by themselves.

Selective prediction

Calibration and abstention literature supplies proper scoring rules and risk–coverage evaluation for uncertain reference.

Nearest · May 2026Gaze-conditioned VLA

Gaze2Act: Gaze-Conditioned Vision-Language-Action Policies for Interactive Robot Manipulation — Zuo et al.

Cross-view semantic gaze grounding, object contour/heatmap prompting, and an action-head conditioning branch; evaluated on object, part, compositional, and dynamic intent on real Unitree G1. It removes any “first gaze-conditioned VLA” claim and motivates direct architectural/uncertainty comparison.

Preprint · March 2026Attention supervision

Gaze-Regularized Vision-Language-Action Models for Robotic Manipulation — Pani & Yang

Uses aggregated gaze-like heatmaps to regularize internal VLA attention during training without eye tracking at inference. Relevant to representation learning, but different from live Tobii reference disambiguation. Verify empirical artifacts and peer-review status before relying on claims.

Near neighbor · April 2026Human intention prior

GazeVLA: Learning Human Intention for Robotic Manipulation — Li et al.

Pretrains on egocentric human data and models gaze as an intention representation before robot action generation. It concerns transferable human-intention priors; GazePick instead treats live operator gaze as uncertain online evidence about a visible referent.

Near neighbor · January 2026Foundation-model autonomy

Intent at a Glance: Gaze-Guided Robotic Manipulation via Foundation Models (GAMMA) — Tay et al.

Uses egocentric gaze and a vision-language model to infer intent and parameterize modular autonomous manipulation. It supports gaze + foundation-model feasibility while leaving live VLA action adaptation and screen-gaze uncertainty as distinct questions.

2025 preprintWearable gaze

GazeGrasp: DNN-Driven Robotic Grasping with Wearable Eye-Gaze Interface — Shi et al.

Combines wearable gaze with object detection and robotic grasping. It reinforces the need for a non-VLA gaze-to-object baseline and highlights calibration, fixation, and false-activation issues.

January 2026Assistive arm

Eye-Tracking-Driven Control in Daily Task Assistance for Assistive Robotic Arms — Fischer-Janzen et al.

Addresses 3D gaze accuracy and task ambiguity through object/task selection in an assistive framework. Useful for failure lessons and interpretable object selection, not evidence for VLA necessity.

2022Robot Transformer

RT-1: Robotics Transformer for Real-World Control at Scale — Brohan et al.

Establishes large-scale language-conditioned real-robot behavior learning and tokenized action output. Provides historical foundation, not a direct gaze baseline.

Paper ↗
2023VLA

RT-2: Vision-Language-Action Models Transfer Web Knowledge to Robotic Control — Brohan et al.

Casts robot actions as text tokens in a co-fine-tuned vision-language model. Establishes the VLA formulation and semantic transfer motivation.

Official project/paper ↗
2024Open VLA

OpenVLA: An Open-Source Vision-Language-Action Model — Kim et al.

A 7B model pretrained on 970k Open X-Embodiment episodes. Official code supports custom RLDS/PyTorch data and LoRA; it is the primary candidate base model and documents the practical compute constraints.

2025Efficient adaptation

Fine-Tuning Vision-Language-Action Models: Optimizing Speed and Success (OpenVLA-OFT) — Kim et al.

Introduces continuous action prediction, action chunks, and an optimized fine-tuning recipe with faster inference and strong robot adaptation. The recommended current path if its action contract and compute fit xArm.

Official project/paper/code ↗
Near neighbor · March 2026Visual prompting

VP-VLA: Visual Prompting as an Interface for Vision-Language-Action Models — Wang et al.

Separates high-level planning from low-level execution through crosshairs and bounding boxes overlaid on the observation, with an auxiliary visual-grounding objective. It strengthens the case for an explicit spatial interface while removing novelty from “draw a cue for a VLA.”

Paper ↗ · Official project ↗
Near neighbor · December 2025Referent prompting

Point What You Mean: Visually Grounded Instruction Policy (Point-VLA) — Yu et al.

Adds explicit boxes to VLA inputs for cluttered, unseen-object, and precise-placement reference, and compares visual prompt forms. GazePick must contribute the uncertain human-to-region inference and selective repair that precede any such box.

Paper ↗
2023Imitation learning

Diffusion Policy: Visuomotor Policy Learning via Action Diffusion — Chi et al.

A strong data-efficient policy for narrow manipulation. OpenVLA’s own results note that narrow single-instruction tasks can favor Diffusion Policy; it is an important baseline if GazePick trains low-level actions.

Official project/paper/code ↗
2024Generalist policy

Octo: An Open-Source Generalist Robot Policy — Octo Model Team

An open generalist policy trained on diverse robot data. Relevant as a transferable-policy comparison and for dataset/action standardization.

Open X-Embodiment: Robotic Learning Datasets and RT-X Models — Open X-Embodiment Collaboration

Combines 1M+ trajectories across 22 embodiments and 60 datasets in standardized form. It motivates pretraining and RLDS, while making embodiment/action normalization an explicit xArm adaptation issue.

DROID: A Large-Scale In-the-Wild Robot Manipulation Dataset — Khazatsky et al.

Provides diverse real-world robot manipulation episodes, standardized collection hardware, multiple views, and rich metadata. It informs collection discipline rather than replacing xArm demonstrations.

Simple Open-Vocabulary Object Detection with Vision Transformers (OWL-ViT) — Minderer et al.

Supports text-conditioned candidate detection. Useful when part categories vary, but detector confidence is not human-intent confidence.

Grounding DINO: Marrying DINO with Grounded Pre-Training for Open-Set Object Detection — Liu et al.

Links language queries to boxes and can generate candidates for an open-vocabulary scene. Use as a candidate generator, with versioned thresholds and manually audited masks.

Segment Anything — Kirillov et al.

Provides promptable masks that can define candidate AOIs. It does not identify which object the user intends, so gaze/language fusion and uncertainty remain necessary.

Team reading order

Read for decisions, not accumulation

Each reading session should end with a one-page decision note: what the paper establishes, what it does not establish, and which design choice changes.

Gaze2Act + GazeVLA + GAMMA + GazeGrasp

Map exact overlap in gaze source, whether gaze is online or supervisory, grounding, action integration, robot, task, uncertainty, and human confirmation.

Point-VLA + VP-VLA + REI-Bench

Separate the value of an explicit visual prompt from the harder problem of inferring that prompt from vague language and noisy human gaze.

Calibration + selective prediction

Choose proper scores, risk–coverage evaluation, validation-only threshold selection, clarification cost, and held-out corruption tests.

OpenVLA + OFT + Diffusion Policy

Choose the action interface, compute plan, and narrow-task reference. Reproduce inference only after Stage A is viable and before collecting adaptation demonstrations.

Open X-Embodiment + DROID

Freeze the episode schema, action normalization, collection protocol, and leakage-safe grouping.

xarm_ros2 + exact Tobii documentation

Resolve implementation versions, licensing, timestamps, hardware limits, and safety controls on the actual devices.
