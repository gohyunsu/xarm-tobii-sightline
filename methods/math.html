Mathematical specification · versionable and falsifiable

Every threshold needs
a variable, unit, and failure state.

This page turns the verbal proposal into an auditable mathematical object. It distinguishes a gaze measurement from a latent mental state, separates visibility from inspection, defines the geometry-only and gaze-informed policies, and states the randomized estimand that supports—or falsifies—the incremental value of gaze.

1 · Notation and units

Define the observational unit before the equations

An episode is indexed by participant \(i\), scene \(s\), task \(k\), interface condition \(c\), and continuous common time \(t\). Device timestamps are never treated as common time until clock mapping is audited.

\(c\in\mathcal C\)interface condition\(\mathcal C=\{0,1,2,3\}\): manual, persistent, geometry-only, Sightline
\(v\in\mathcal V\)camera view\(\mathcal V=\{O,S,W\}\): overhead, side, wrist
\(q(t)\)task phaseselection, approach, pre-contact, contact, transport, placement, retreat

2 · Measurement model

A reported point is represented with uncertainty

The tracker returns a point estimate and validity information. The study adds an uncertainty model estimated from session validation—not from task outcomes.

Screen-gaze observation
\[ Z_n \mid \mu_n,\Sigma_n,m_n=1 \;\sim\; \mathcal N_2(\mu_n,\Sigma_n), \qquad \mu_n=\begin{bmatrix}x_n\\y_n\end{bmatrix}. \tag{1} \]

The covariance \(\Sigma_n\) may begin as a session-level estimate derived from validation residuals. A later model may depend on head position or screen region, but its parameters must be estimated independently of condition outcomes. Invalid samples have \(m_n=0\) and contribute no pseudo-location.

Rendered AOI
\[ \widetilde P_{a,v}(t) =H_{v\rightarrow \mathrm{screen}}(t)\,P_{a,v}(t), \tag{2} \]

\(H_{v\rightarrow \mathrm{screen}}(t)\) includes the actual panel rectangle, crop, scaling, and layout at render time. If view \(v\) is not rendered, \(\widetilde P_{a,v}(t)=\varnothing\). This prevents an AOI in an invisible tab from receiving gaze coverage.

Uncertainty-aware overlap
\[ p_{n,a,v} =\Pr\!\left(Z_n\in\widetilde P_{a,v}(t_n^{(g)})\right) =\int_{\widetilde P_{a,v}(t_n^{(g)})} \phi_2(z;\mu_n,\Sigma_n)\,dz . \tag{3} \]

The integral can be approximated numerically or compared with hard-hit and dilated-polygon analyses. The analysis reports whether conclusions survive reasonable alternatives for \(\Sigma_n\) and AOI boundary dilation.

Construct boundary: \(p_{n,a,v}\) is a probability of geometric overlap under a measurement model. It is not a probability of attention, comprehension, intent, consent, or safety.

3 · Inspection coverage

Combine recency, overlap, visibility, and valid-sample mass

Coverage summarizes limited recent evidence that a required visual surface was inspected while it was actually visible. It remains undefined when the quality gate fails.

Recency kernel
\[ \kappa(\Delta t)= \begin{cases} \exp(-\Delta t/\tau_g), & 0\le \Delta t\le W,\\ 0, & \text{otherwise}, \end{cases} \tag{4} \]
Windowed coverage for one AOI
\[ C_a(t;W)= \frac{ \displaystyle\sum_{v\in\mathcal V} \sum_{n}\, m_n\,\kappa(t-t_n^{(g)})\, p_{n,a,v}\, \nu_{a,v}(t_n^{(g)}) }{ \displaystyle\sum_n m_n\,\kappa(t-t_n^{(g)})+\varepsilon }. \tag{5} \]

The numerator requires four things at once: a valid sample, temporal relevance, uncertain overlap with the rendered AOI, and visible task evidence. The denominator represents available valid gaze mass in the same window. \(\varepsilon\) prevents numerical division by zero but does not make a low-quality window valid.

Quality gate
\[ \rho_g(t;W)= \frac{\sum_n m_n\,\ind{0\le t-t_n^{(g)}\le W}} {\sum_n \ind{0\le t-t_n^{(g)}\le W}}, \qquad Q_g(t)=\rho_g(t;W)\exp\!\left[-\frac{e_{\mathrm{cal}}^2}{2\sigma_Q^2}\right]. \tag{6} \]

\(e_{\mathrm{cal}}\) is the latest validation error in visual angle or pixels, consistently converted. If \(Q_g(t)<\tau_Q\), \(C_a(t;W)\) is not interpreted; C3 falls back to C2.

Phase-level deficit
\[ a^\star(t)=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{a\in\mathcal A_k(q(t))}\omega_a(q(t)), \qquad D(t)=1-C_{a^\star(t)}(t;W). \tag{7} \]

The simplest confirmatory policy selects one preregistered highest-value AOI \(a^\star\) per task phase. If multiple AOIs are jointly required, replace Equation (7) with a prespecified weighted minimum or conjunction—never choose the aggregation after observing outcomes.

4 · Geometric risk

Normalize interpretable physical components

Risk describes urgency for additional visual evidence. It is not a probability of injury or a certified collision predictor.

Distance and approach rate
\[ d(t)=\min_{x\in G(t),\,y\in S_{a^\star}(t)}\lVert x-y\rVert_2, \qquad \dot d(t)=\frac{d(t)-d(t-\Delta t)}{\Delta t}, \tag{8} \]

\(G(t)\) is the calibrated gripper geometry and \(S_{a^\star}(t)\) the task-critical surface. Positive closure speed is \(v_{\mathrm{close}}(t)=\max[-\dot d(t),0]\).

Time to contact
\[ \TTC(t)= \begin{cases} \dfrac{d(t)}{v_{\mathrm{close}}(t)}, & v_{\mathrm{close}}(t)>\epsilon_v,\\[6pt] +\infty, & \text{otherwise}. \end{cases} \tag{9} \]
Normalized components
\[ \begin{aligned} r_d(t) &= \clip\!\left(1-\frac{d(t)}{d_0},\,0,\,1\right),\\ r_T(t) &= \exp[-\TTC(t)/\tau_T],\\ r_O(t) &= 1-\frac{A_{\mathrm{visible}}(a^\star,O,t)} {A_{\mathrm{projected}}(a^\star,O,t)+\varepsilon_A},\\ r_q(t) &\in [0,1]. \end{aligned} \tag{10} \]

\(r_O\) uses the current main view \(O\) and the task-critical surface, not the whole object box. \(r_q\) is a preregistered phase gate: for example, near zero during target preview and high during pre-contact.

Convex risk score
\[ R(t)=w_d r_d(t)+w_T r_T(t)+w_O r_O(t)+w_q r_q(t), \quad w_j\ge0,\quad\sum_j w_j=1. \tag{11} \]

The simplex constraint keeps \(R(t)\in[0,1]\) and makes component weights interpretable. Raw component values, normalizers, weights, and policy version are stored for every trigger.

5 · Auxiliary-view selection

Choose the view that reveals evidence at the lowest switching cost

Trigger eligibility and view selection are different problems. The first asks whether assistance is warranted; the second asks which available feed is informative and operationally stable.

Candidate-view utility
\[ U_v(t)= \nu_{a^\star,v}(t) -\lambda_S\,S_v(t) -\lambda_L\,L_v(t) -\lambda_F\,F_v(t), \tag{12} \]

\(S_v\) is a prespecified switch/reorientation cost, \(L_v\) a normalized latency or frame-age penalty, and \(F_v\) a dropout/staleness penalty. Coefficients are nonnegative and frozen after pilot.

Feasible set and selected view
\[ \mathcal V_{\mathrm{ok}}(t)= \left\{v\in\mathcal V\setminus\{v_{\mathrm{main}}\}: \nu_{a^\star,v}(t)\ge\tau_\nu,\; \operatorname{age}_v(t)\le\tau_{\mathrm{age}} \right\}, \qquad v^\star(t)=\argmax_{v\in\mathcal V_{\mathrm{ok}}(t)}U_v(t). \tag{13} \]

If \(\mathcal V_{\mathrm{ok}}(t)=\varnothing\), the system does not display a low-quality feed as if it solved the visibility problem. It logs “no adequate auxiliary evidence.”

6 · Eligibility and finite-state policy

Thresholds require persistence and hysteresis

A raw threshold checked every frame would flicker. The deployed object is a finite-state machine with onset persistence, separate release thresholds, minimum display duration, and cooldown.

Condition-specific eligibility
\[ \begin{aligned} E_2(t)&=\ind{R(t)\ge\tau_R^+}\, \ind{\mathcal V_{\mathrm{ok}}(t)\ne\varnothing},\\ E_3(t)&=E_2(t)\, \ind{C_{a^\star}(t;W)<\tau_C^-}\, \ind{Q_g(t)\ge\tau_Q}. \end{aligned} \tag{14} \]

C2 uses geometry only. C3 uses exactly the same risk and candidate-view machinery, then adds the inspection-deficit and quality gates. This identity is essential for the C3−C2 ablation.

State transitions
\[ \begin{array}{lll} \mathrm{OFF}\rightarrow\mathrm{ON} &\text{if}& E_c(t)=1\text{ continuously for }T_{\mathrm{on}},\\[2pt] \mathrm{ON}\rightarrow\mathrm{COOLDOWN} &\text{if}& t-t_{\mathrm{on}}\ge T_{\min} \text{ and } \bigl[R(t)<\tau_R^- \;\lor\; C_{a^\star}(t;W)\ge\tau_C^+\bigr],\\[2pt] \mathrm{COOLDOWN}\rightarrow\mathrm{OFF} &\text{if}& t-t_{\mathrm{off}}\ge T_{\mathrm{cd}}. \end{array} \tag{15} \]

Hysteresis requires \(\tau_R^-<\tau_R^+\) and \(\tau_C^-<\tau_C^+\). Manual close, pin, and view request are separate explicit transitions and are always logged. A physical stop bypasses the UI state machine entirely.

7 · Clock model

Map device time; preserve residual error

Gaze, video, browser render, controller, and robot clocks can differ in offset and drift. Causal ordering is credible only if the mapping error is small relative to the decision window.

Affine clock mapping
\[ t^{(\mathrm{common})}_{d,j} =\alpha_d+\beta_d\,t^{(\mathrm{device})}_{d,j} +e_{d,j}, \tag{16} \]

\(\alpha_d\) is offset, \(\beta_d\) drift/scale, and \(e_{d,j}\) residual error for device \(d\). Source time and mapped time are both stored.

Session synchronization gate
\[ q_{0.95}\!\left(\lvert e_{d,j}\rvert\right)\le\varepsilon_{\mathrm{sync}} \quad\forall d, \qquad \lvert\widehat\beta_d-1\rvert\le\varepsilon_{\mathrm{drift}}. \tag{17} \]

The tolerances are chosen relative to \(T_{\mathrm{on}}\), camera frame period, and hypothesized pre-contact interval. If the gate fails, online C3 is disabled and analysis remains shadow mode.

8 · Outcomes

Predefine the trial-level fault without hiding components

The primary physical endpoint is a binary task fault. Its components remain separately reported so a reduced “any fault” rate cannot hide a shift from minor contact to severe drop.

Primary physical endpoint
\[ Y^{\mathrm{fault}}_{iskr} = \ind{ \text{forbidden contact} \;\lor\;\text{drop} \;\lor\;\text{wrong grasp} \;\lor\;\text{failed insertion} \;\lor\;\text{deviation}>\delta_k }. \tag{18} \]

Task-specific contact regions and tolerances \(\delta_k\) are fixed in the scene manifest. Completion time, path length, jerk, inter-panel transitions, override, camera churn, and NASA-TLX remain distinct secondary outcomes.

Visual switching rate
\[ Y^{\mathrm{switch}}_{iskr} =\frac{N_{\mathrm{panel\;transitions}}} {T_{\mathrm{active}}/60\ \mathrm{s}}, \tag{19} \]

Normalize by active teleoperation time rather than total trial duration, excluding instruction and rating screens. Report the raw count and duration as well.

9 · Randomized estimands and models

Estimate the condition effect, then analyze the mechanism

Condition is randomized within participant using a balanced order. The primary estimand is intention-to-treat at the assigned display policy. Conditioning on realized gaze eligibility can introduce post-randomization selection and is not the primary causal claim.

Primary high-occlusion intention-to-treat estimand
\[ \Delta_{3-2}^{\mathrm{fault},H} = \E\!\left[ Y^{\mathrm{fault}}(3)-Y^{\mathrm{fault}}(2) \mid O=H \right]. \tag{20a} \]

For a beneficial effect on faults, \(\Delta_{3-2}^{\mathrm{fault},H}<0\). The analogous switching estimand uses \(Y^{\mathrm{switch}}\). Low-occlusion estimates test whether the policy avoids unnecessary intervention.

First-opportunity pre-cue stratum
\[ \begin{aligned} t_R &=\inf\!\left\{t:R(t)\ge\tau_R^+,\ \mathcal V_{\mathrm{ok}}(t)\ne\varnothing\right\},\\ Z^{\mathrm{pre}} &= \ind{C_{a^\star}(t_R^-;W)<\tau_C^-}\, \ind{Q_g(t_R^-)\ge\tau_Q},\\ \Delta_{3-2}^{\mathrm{fault},H,\mathrm{pre}} &= \E\!\left[ Y^{\mathrm{fault}}(3)-Y^{\mathrm{fault}}(2) \mid O=H,\ Z^{\mathrm{pre}}=1 \right]. \end{aligned} \tag{20b} \]

Equation (20b) is a targeted confirmatory or key-secondary estimand for the first geometry-risk opportunity. \(Z^{\mathrm{pre}}\) is measured at the left limit \(t_R^-\), before either C2 or C3 renders its first adaptive cue. It is defensible only when C2 and C3 are verified to have identical within-trial display history up to \(t_R\). Trials that violate that condition remain in the intention-to-treat estimand (20a) but not this pre-cue stratum.

Mixed-effects logistic model
\[ \logit\Pr\!\left(Y^{\mathrm{fault}}_{iskr}=1\right) = \beta_0+\boldsymbol\beta_C^\top\mathbf C_{iskr} +\beta_O O_{iskr} +\boldsymbol\gamma^\top(\mathbf C_{iskr}O_{iskr}) +\boldsymbol\beta_X^\top\mathbf X_{iskr} +b_i+u_s, \tag{21} \] \[ b_i\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma_b^2), \qquad u_s\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma_u^2). \tag{22} \]

\(\mathbf X\) includes task, trial order, and prespecified experience terms. With C2 as the reference condition and \(O=1\) for high occlusion, the log-odds H3 contrast is \(\beta_{C3}+\gamma_{C3}\). Fit the pre-cue-stratum analysis with the same treatment contrast on the prespecified \(Z^{\mathrm{pre}}=1\) subset, while retaining (20a) as the broader randomized effect. Report marginal probability differences in addition to odds ratios.

Performance-preserving visual-cost criterion
\[ \Delta_{3-1}^{\mathrm{success}}>-\delta_{\mathrm{NI}} \quad\text{and}\quad \Delta_{3-1}^{\mathrm{switch}}<0. \tag{23} \]

Equation (23) formalizes “retain useful multiview performance while reducing visual cost.” The non-inferiority margin \(\delta_{\mathrm{NI}}\) must be scientifically justified before collection. It is not selected from the observed confidence interval.

Outcome-specific families

  • Binary fault/success: logistic mixed model with marginal contrasts.
  • Contact or transition counts: negative-binomial or Poisson mixed model after dispersion checks, with exposure offset when appropriate.
  • Positive skewed time/path: log-normal or Gamma mixed model after preregistered diagnostics.
  • Repeated ratings: ordinal or approximately continuous mixed model, explicitly labeled secondary.

Mechanism analysis

Pre-cue coverage, cue latency, and operator response can test the proposed mechanism. Because gaze behavior and display history are time-varying and condition affected, such analyses are secondary unless a formal longitudinal causal model is preregistered. “Trials where Sightline decided to help” are not a randomized subgroup.

10 · Assumptions and sensitivity

Write down what can break the mathematics

Equations do not create validity. Their assumptions determine which interpretations survive real sensor and behavioral data.

A1 · Calibration

Robot, camera, screen, and gaze mappings are stable within the audit interval. Sensitivity: exclude high residual sessions and vary AOI dilation.

A2 · Positivity

Each participant can encounter every randomized condition and both occlusion levels safely. Otherwise the target population or task must narrow.

A3 · Limited carryover

Balanced order, practice, and order terms reduce learning/carryover. Residual carryover is examined rather than assumed absent.

A4 · Missing gaze

Invalid gaze is not recoded as absence of inspection. Primary ITT retains randomized trials; quality-based analyses are transparent sensitivity analyses.

A5 · Stable scoring

Fault regions and tolerances are versioned, condition-masked, and checked for inter-rater agreement.

A6 · No hidden motion path

Only the guarded controller gateway can command xArm. Software authority is reviewed and tested.

A7 · Frozen policy

Weights, windows, thresholds, and FSM timings are frozen after pilot and before confirmatory outcomes.

A8 · Scoped generality

Results apply to the audited xArm, camera geometry, lightweight tasks, operator sample, and quality range—not industrial safety broadly.
