Team playbook · July–November 2026

Build a vertical slice, then deepen the evidence.

The schedule protects one demonstrable path—look, speak, confirm, guarded pick-and-place—while model adaptation, user study, and industrial scenario work proceed behind explicit gates. Every phase ends with an artifact that another team member can reproduce.

01 · North star

One sentence everyone can test

Team decisions should shorten the path to the same observable outcome. Additions that do not improve this episode, its evidence, or its robustness move to the backlog.

An operator looks at one of several similar parts in the robot-camera view, says its destination, verifies the system’s uncertainty-aware proposal, and a guarded xArm places the correct part while the episode is fully replayable.

Hackathon value

A legible human–AI–robot interaction tied to wrong-pick and workflow cost in high-mix manufacturing/logistics.

Research value

A matched test of whether gaze helps, whether VLA helps beyond classical grounding, and whether adaptation helps beyond prompting.

Partner value

A concrete interface where process scenarios, object selection, UI/data visualization, and fixture/safety expertise materially change the outcome.

03 · Parallel workstreams

Separate ownership; integrate weekly

Each workstream owns interfaces and tests, not a private end-to-end fork. One shared episode ID and schema connect all teams.

WS-A

Gaze & interaction

  • Tobii SDK, calibration, mapping
  • reference window and quality
  • operator UI and repair states
  • click oracle and usability measures
WS-B

Perception & fusion

  • candidate masks and ground truth
  • language parser/ASR
  • posterior, calibration, abstention
  • offline replay benchmark
WS-C

VLA & data

  • base model reproduction
  • episode adapter and action contract
  • frozen prompt, LoRA/OFT
  • model card and checkpoint lock
WS-D

xArm & evaluation

  • workspace, skills, guard, faults
  • state/action logging
  • scene randomization and labels
  • matched trials and analysis

Core team contribution

Tobii acquisition/calibration, gaze–language grounding, VLA adaptation, xArm control, synchronized data, and quantitative evaluation.

Matched team contribution

Regional manufacturing/logistics requirements, representative objects and scenarios, operator/data visualization, and safety/fixture design. Both teams co-design scenes, collect demonstrations, and interpret comparisons.

Integration rhythm: merge to one main branch; run a 30-minute hardware integration at least twice weekly; archive one replayable episode and one known failure after each session; keep model checkpoints and raw data outside Git.

05 · First two sprints

Concrete tasks for the next ten working days

The first objective is a replayable, guarded vertical slice. Model novelty waits until sensing and robot execution are trustworthy.

Sprint 1 · days 1–5

Freeze and bring up

  1. inventory exact devices, firmware, SDK, OS, GPU, and rights;
  2. freeze V1 objects, bins, camera pose, grammar, and confirmation;
  3. define message schemas, episode IDs, clocks, and repository boundaries;
  4. bring up xArm home/stop/gripper and reduced workspace;
  5. render fixed camera feed and log exact image rectangle;
  6. capture Tobii samples and independent validation markers;
  7. record one clicked-target dry-run episode.
Sprint 2 · days 6–10

Close the first loop

  1. implement candidate masks and scene ground truth;
  2. align gaze window to push-to-talk;
  3. compute candidate gaze mass, posterior, margin, and abstention;
  4. build proposal/confirm/clarify UI states;
  5. connect accepted candidate to the guarded pick/place skill;
  6. inject stale gaze, bad target, out-of-workspace, and E-stop faults;
  7. replay and score 20 engineering episodes.
Day-10 acceptance: a new team member can load a scene, calibrate, issue a deictic command, see either a correct proposal or a specific abstention, confirm, complete one physical pick-and-place, and replay the episode without reading developer memory.

06 · Shared study curriculum

Six sessions tied to design decisions

Rotate presenters. Each session produces a short decision record and at least one change, test, or confirmed non-change in the project.

What is a VLA?

Read RT-2, OpenVLA, and OFT. Decide base policy, action interface, control rate, GPU feasibility, and the boundary between learned policy and guarded skill.

What can gaze establish?

Read Tobii technical material, GazeGrasp, and assistive gaze work. Decide calibration metric, mapping test, reference window, and prohibited intent claims.

What is already novel?

Read Gaze2Act and GAMMA in full. Populate an overlap table for input view, grounding, conditioning, robot, tasks, baselines, uncertainty, and user interaction.

How should demonstrations be stored?

Read Open X-Embodiment and DROID. Freeze episode schema, action normalization, split groups, quality report, and dataset manifest.

How should uncertainty act?

Read calibration and selective-prediction sources. Decide scores, risk–coverage plots, threshold tuning, clarification messages, and test corruptions.

What can the final evidence say?

Review mixed-effects analysis, outcome hierarchy, sample simulation, negative-result rules, ethics, media provenance, and the final claim matrix.

09 · Definition of done

Four deliverable bundles

Finishing means another person can understand, run, inspect, and challenge the work.

System bundle

  • one-command services
  • versioned configs
  • preflight/fault tests
  • guard and restart guide
  • demo scene manifest

Data bundle

  • schema and examples
  • quality reports
  • split manifest
  • derivation scripts
  • privacy/license decision

Evidence bundle

  • B0–B4 result table
  • risk–coverage/calibration
  • mixed-model or interval report
  • failure gallery
  • negative findings

Presentation bundle

  • three-minute live script
  • backup video
  • architecture animation
  • poster/deck
  • source/provenance page
Final review question: can a skeptical evaluator see the ambiguous scene, understand Tobii’s limited role, observe a correct abstention, trace the xArm safety boundary, and read the comparison that establishes—or rejects—the VLA contribution?
